# Binomial Approximation
Binomial: $X\sim B(n,p)$  
where $q=1-p$ , the probability of failure, and $Var(X)=npq$

When sample size n moves to values such that $np>5$ and $nq>5$

$$
X\sim B(n,p)\text{ becomes }Y\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\text{ where }\mu=np\text{ and }\sigma^{2}=npq
$$

Continuity Correction:
- used to account for rounding of continuous values to discrete values. 
	- **Continuity correction is intended to adjust the continuous approximation (normal distribution) when estimating probabilities for discrete values (like binomial).**
- Adjustments to probability calculation:
	- IF $P(X=a)$ use $P(a-0.5<X<a+0.5)$
	- IF $P(X>a)$ use $P(X>a+0.5)$
	- IF $P(X\leq a)$ use $P(X<a+0.5)$
	- IF $P(X<a)$ use $P(X<a-0.5)$
	- IF $P(X\geq a)$ use $P(X>a-0.5)$

Example:
In a particular school of Students are men and are women. Given a sample of 50 students, what is the probability that more than half of them are women?
- $np=50\times 0.4=20>5$
- $nq=50\times 0.6=30>5$
- We can approximate X with normal distribution $Y\sim N(\mu,\sigma^{2})$
	- $\mu=np=20$
	- $\sigma^{2}=npq=50\times 0.4\times 0.6=12$
	- $Y\sim N(20,12)$
- Probability that more than half of them are women? $P(X>25)?$
	- $P(X>25)=P(Y>25.5)$
	- Use Z conversion formula: $P\left( \frac{Z>(25.5-20)}{\sqrt{ 12 }} \right)=P(Z>1.59)$
	- Use Z-table: $P(Z>1.59)=1-P(Z\leq 1.59)=1-0.9441=0.0559$

# Combining Random Variables
Adding or subtracting random variables
1. Add the expected values:
	- $E(X+Y)=E(X)+E(Y)$
2. Subtract the expected values:
	- $E(X-Y)=E(X)-E(Y)$
3. Add the variances **(when X and Y are dependent variables)**
	- $Var(X+Y)=Var(X)+Var(Y)+2Cov(X,Y)$
4. Subtract the variances **(when X and Y are dependent variables)**
	- $Var(X-Y)=Var(X)+Var(Y)-2Cov(X,Y)$

- Note: $Var(X+Y)\neq Var(X)+Var(Y)$

Definition:

$$
\begin{gathered}
E(aX\pm b)=aE(X)\pm b\\
Var(aX\pm b)=a^{2}Var(X)\\
\color{white} \rule{6cm}{0.4pt}\\
E(aX\pm bY)=aE(X)\pm bE(Y)\\
Var(AX\pm bY)=a^{2}Var(X)+b^{2}Var(Y)\text{ where X and Y are independent variables}
\end{gathered}
$$

<font color="#00b0f0">Note X and Y have to be **independent** variables</font>:
- If X and Y are **normal**, then the resultant $aX\pm bY$ is also **normal**
- If X and Y are **binomial**, then the resultant $aX+bY$ is **binomial**, <font color="#00b0f0">only if they have the **same** success probability p</font>.
Note: subtracting 2 independent variables results in **more variability** in the outcome because we are combining 2 uncertainties from both variables. 
- The resulting difference can vary more dramatically than either of the original variables alone

Example:
A company offers a buyer's insurance policy that costs a customer $70 dollar. The company will make a pay out of $10,000 dollars to the customer if they are victim to fraud in that year. The company set this price based on the probability of fraud being 0.002.  
Let X represent the company's profit from one of these policies. The table below displays the probability distribution and summary statistics for x.

|          | No Fraud           | Fraud |
| -------- | ------------------ | ----- |
| X=Profit | 70                 | -9930 |
| P(X)     | 0.998              | 0.002 |
|          | Mean               | $50   |
|          | Standard Deviation | ~ 447 |
What if we add $10 to our expected net profit of $50?
- E(X)+10
- $E(aX\pm b)=a\times E(X)\pm b=1\times 50+10=60$
- <font color="#00b0f0">Standard deviation does not change when adding or subtracting</font>
	- $\sigma_{Y}=\sigma_{X}\approx 447$

Example:
Suppose the time for Meg to travel from her home to the airport is normally distributed with $\mu = 25$ mins and $\sigma = 3$ mins,  
and the time for her to clear the airport screening to boarding the plane is normally distributed with $\mu=15$ mins and $\sigma=2$ mins.
- Given that Meg leaves home for 45 mins before the last call to board. What is the probability that she will miss her flight? We want to know probability of total time: ((Time to airport + Time to screening)>45)
	- $W\sim N(25,3^{2})$
		- E(W) = 25
		- $\sigma^{2}=3^{2}$
	- $X\sim N(15,2^{2})$
		- E(X) = 15
		- $\sigma^{2}=2^{2}$
	- The combined normal mean: $E(Y)=E(aX+ bW)=aE(X)+ bE(W)$
		- $E(Y)=E(X)+E(W)=25+15=40$
	- The combined normal Variance: $Var(aX+ bW)=a^{2}Var(X)+b^{2}Var(W)$
		- $Var(Y)=Var(X)+Var(W)=3^{2}+2^{2}=13$
	- Once we have combined normal mean & variance and Y inequality, we can convert to Z value to find probability
		- $Z=\frac{Y-\mu}{\sigma}=\frac{45-40}{\sqrt{ 13 }}\approx 1.39$
		- $P(Y>45)=P(Z>1.39)=1-P(Z<1.39)=1-0.9177=0.0823$
